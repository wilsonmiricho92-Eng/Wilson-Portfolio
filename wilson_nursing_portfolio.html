Nurse-Driven CAUTI Prevention Bundle: A Practice Needs Assessment
NU672 · DNP Scholarly Project · Memorial Hospital West · APA 7th Edition
Evidence-Based Practice
Catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTIs) represent one of the most prevalent and preventable hospital-acquired infections in acute care settings, accounting for approximately 36% of all healthcare-associated infections in the United States (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], 2023). At Memorial Hospital West, a retrospective chart review revealed a CAUTI rate of 2.8 per 1,000 catheter-days during the preceding fiscal year—exceeding the National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) benchmark of 1.5 per 1,000 catheter-days and signaling a critical gap in current nursing practice.
The proposed nurse-driven CAUTI prevention bundle operationalizes the ABCDE framework: Aseptic insertion technique, Bundle compliance audits, Catheter necessity reassessment every 24 hours, Daily perineal care protocols, and Early removal criteria guided by evidence-based indications. Rooted in the Iowa Model of Evidence-Based Practice, this initiative positions bedside nurses as autonomous decision-makers in catheter management, thereby reducing physician-dependent order delays and aligning institutional practice with current NHSN guidelines.

Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders: Clinical Presentation and Evidence-Based Management
NUR 672 · PMHNP Psychotherapy · Purdue University Global
Psychiatric Nursing
Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) occupies a distinct diagnostic category within the DSM-5-TR, characterized by four core symptom clusters following exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence: intrusion symptoms, persistent avoidance, negative alterations in cognition and mood, and marked alterations in arousal and reactivity (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). For the psychiatric-mental health nurse practitioner (PMHNP), accurate differential diagnosis requires systematic exclusion of acute stress disorder, adjustment disorder, and complex PTSD presentations, each of which carries divergent treatment implications.
First-line pharmacological management integrates selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)—specifically sertraline and paroxetine, the only FDA-approved medications for PTSD—alongside trauma-focused psychotherapeutic modalities such as Prolonged Exposure (PE) and Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT). Emerging evidence supports the adjunctive use of prazosin for trauma-related nightmares; however, the PMHNP must weigh cardiovascular risk profiles, particularly in older adults, prior to initiation (Raskind et al., 2018).

Antihypertensive and Respiratory Agents Across the Lifespan: A Pharmacology Reference
NU553 · Advanced Pharmacology · Unit 7 Assignment
Pharmacology
Lisinopril, an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor, remains a cornerstone of antihypertensive therapy in adults with co-occurring diabetes mellitus or chronic kidney disease due to its renoprotective mechanism of action. The drug's inhibition of ACE reduces angiotensin II production, leading to vasodilation and decreased aldosterone secretion. Clinically, the PMHNP must monitor serum potassium and creatinine levels biannually, as ACE inhibitors carry a risk of hyperkalemia and acute kidney injury, particularly in patients receiving concurrent NSAID therapy or potassium-sparing diuretics (Woo & Robinson, 2020).
In the pediatric population, dosing of antihypertensive agents demands weight-based calculations with strict upper limits; amlodipine at 0.1–0.3 mg/kg/day (max 5 mg/day in children aged 6–17) represents a preferred calcium channel blocker given its favorable tolerability profile and once-daily dosing schedule, which supports adherence in school-age patients. Across all lifespan stages, the advanced practice nurse should systematically assess for drug-drug interactions, organ-specific toxicity, and patient-centered barriers to adherence.

Washington State SB 6071: Expanding Mental Health Services and Nursing Scope of Practice
Healthcare Policy & Advocacy · Team Presentation · May 2025
Health Policy
Washington State Senate Bill 6071 represents a pivotal legislative response to the state's escalating mental health crisis, which saw psychiatric emergency department visits increase by 34% between 2019 and 2023—a trajectory accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic and exacerbated by persistent workforce shortages in community behavioral health settings. The bill's principal provisions authorize PMHNPs to practice independently without physician oversight agreements, expand Medicaid reimbursement for telehealth-delivered psychiatric services, and allocate $42 million toward crisis stabilization unit infrastructure in rural and underserved counties.
Opposition from organized medicine centers on patient safety concerns in high-acuity presentations; however, a comprehensive evidence synthesis by the National Academy of Medicine (2021) found no statistically significant difference in adverse outcomes between APRN-led and physician-led psychiatric care in ambulatory and community settings. The policy implication is clear: legislative barriers to full practice authority perpetuate inequitable access to mental health services, disproportionately affecting communities of color, rural populations, and individuals experiencing homelessness.
